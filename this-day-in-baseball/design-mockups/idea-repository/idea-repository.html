1. Chosen now: ballpark pages

One page for every park that ever hosted a major league game. It starts from your hand-inked park drawing and adds the real numbers underneath it. You said this idea stands out, so it comes before everything else.

Where it stands

  • Drawings: 77 finished pictures in images/ballparks/ (62 of the 345 listed parks). You are finishing the rest.
  • Checklist: one line per park with the best free photo source, claude.ai/artifact/DAqz3AkbCpQKRDN7mmx6hp
  • Sample page, desktop: claude.ai/artifact/1TNnFK2Cmaa3Z3fszC3rLz
  • Sample page, phone: claude.ai/artifact/7o6XyNeW4GxMx8AXAMPeW6
  • Data behind it: Retrosheet game logs cover 264 parks and 235,607 games. 139 parks have 100 or more games. 204,540 games have a crowd on record and 220,887 have a game length.
  • What the sample shows: by the numbers, a home runs chart, a crowd chart, a big days list. The Polo Grounds beat the league average for home runs in all 49 of its seasons.

The script that rebuilds a park page is design-mockups/ballpark-pages/build_park_mock.py. It takes any park code.

Eight ways to go deeper

1. Park fingerprint

Data on handSize: M

Home runs, runs and crowd size against the league, drawn as a small ink sketch for every park. Fenway and the Polo Grounds would look nothing alike. It works as a thumbnail on the index page.

2. Park timeline

Data on handSize: M

Every tenant, rename and rebuild on one line, so you can see how a city's parks hand off to each other. Park codes never change when a park is renamed, which makes this safe. Could also show which park held the most of a team's history.

3. First and last

Data on handSize: S

First game, last game, first night game and the last big crowd at each park in one box. Retrosheet also publishes a "park firsts" list (park_firsts.htm in the cache) that can check our work.

4. Neighborhood then and now

Needs fetchSize: L

A 1921 aerial photo or a Sanborn fire insurance map from the Library of Congress next to today's coordinates. "What stands where Ebbets Field was" is an easy page to love. Papers and photos from before 1929 are allowed with a credit and a link. Coordinates come from Wikidata and every match needs a hand check, because a search for "Tiger Stadium" once returned a college football stadium.

5. World Series at this park

Data on handSize: S

The World Series game logs are already cached. Each page gets a "Series history" strip: which years, which teams, who won at this park.

6. Park versus park

Data on handSize: M

Pick any two parks and see them side by side: home runs, runs, crowds, years open. A good way to settle bar arguments. Needs a small chooser on the page, and it stays static.

7. Ballpark index

Data on handSize: S

One front page with all the drawings as a grid, sortable by city or era. It becomes the front door for the whole feature and the page people will share. Best first build after the sample page, because it links to every park page.

8. Field dimensions diagram

Needs permissionSize: M

An ink outline of the field with wall distances and heights. The best source is the Seamheads Ballparks Database, which states no reuse terms, so we would ask first. The Diamond Mind database covers every park since 1901 but belongs to a commercial game, so link only. The SportsDataverse file is free but starts around 2001.

Suggested order: ballpark index (7), then park timeline (2), then first and last (3) and World Series (5), which are quick. Neighborhood maps (4) and dimensions (8) need more outside work.

2. Parked data ideas

Fourteen features, all built from data we already own. The ballpark idea was number 4 on the original list, so it is not repeated here. Pitch only: nothing is built until you say yes.

Pictures from the data

Line score as a picture

Data on handSize: S

Every game log has the inning-by-inning line score. Draw it as a small ink bar chart: a tall bar for a big inning, flat for a shutout. The Game of the Day could carry one, and a 12-run inning shows at a glance.

Game-length clock

Data on handSize: S

A clock face or stopwatch strip: "this game took 1:52, the average that year is 3:05." Length is on record for 220,887 games. It suits old games that finished in under two hours.

Attendance crowd

Data on handSize: M

Draw the crowd as dots or a grandstand outline, scaled to the park's own average so a packed house looks packed. Capacity is not in our data, so we compare to the average crowd unless we add a capacity source.

Birthplace map

Needs fetchSize: M

A plain ink map with a dot for where each of the day's players was born. The Retrosheet biodata file lists 27,049 people with birth city and state. We would need a free outline map and a way to place cities. Check the license of any map file before using it.

Family tree cards

Data on handSize: S

The relatives list has 1,329 pairs of brothers, fathers and sons. Show a small card when a pair played on the same day, or when one of them has a milestone. "Aaron and Aaron" is the kind of line readers repeat.

Boxes and features for the day page

Weird endings box

Data on handSize: S

Retrosheet's own lists are cached: forfeits, suspended games, hidden-ball tricks, triple plays, cycles, four home runs in a row, perfect games, no-hitters. A box that says "this has happened only N times" is easy to read and easy to share. Same credit line applies.

Trade wire

Data on handSize: M

The transactions file holds 101,594 trades, signings and releases, each with a date. A "Transactions of the day" box in small agate type fits the 1999 sports-section look. Needs a check that names match our people list.

Umpire and manager cards, "Today in tempers"

Data on handSize: M

The ejections file has 19,780 ejections and the managers file has 859 managers. Show a manager's record that day, an umpire's career games, or a list of that day's ejections.

Day or night

Data on handSize: S

The first night game, the last day game at a park, and the share of games played under lights by decade. A good small chart. The Polo Grounds sample shows 303 night games out of 4,536, about 7 percent.

Weather

Needs fetchSize: M

Temperature, sky, wind and rain from Retrosheet's gameinfo.csv, one file of about 8 MB per year. Solid from 1990 on, thin before 1975 (1925 had temperature for 4 of 1,277 games). Use it only where a value exists and skip the line otherwise.

Negro Leagues same-day strip

Data on handSize: L

8,215 games from Retrosheet's Negro Leagues files are cached (126 MB). A "same day, other league" strip would be strong and meaningful. The files carry a certainty flag called stattype, so we show uncertain numbers honestly. Same credit line, shown prominently.

Rivalry cards

Data on handSize: M

Head-to-head records between two teams as a tug-of-war bar, from the full game logs. Works on any day when the two teams meet.

Reader tools

Time capsule picker

Needs full-year buildSize: M

A reader types a birthday and sees that day. This is the strongest sharing hook on the list. It needs all 366 day files, and only 49 are built so far.

Your team's best day

Data on handSize: M

A reader picks a team and sees every big day for it. The choice is saved in their own browser, with no login, so the site stays static. Needs a team tag on each story.

Keep readers coming back

To discuss

You asked to talk about retention once the to-do list wraps. Starting points: a daily email, share cards, one page per day so search engines can find each day, and more content types such as the ones in this file.

Other sports

To discuss

Expand into other sports, or make a copy of the site for another sport. Data differs by sport. nflverse (NFL, code is MIT, data owned by its owners), balldontlie (NBA only, no key, terms unconfirmed) and the unofficial NHL web API (terms unconfirmed) came up in research. The trade-offs: more readers against double the upkeep.

Per-day pages and hosting

7. Rules that apply to every idea

  • Retrosheet credit stays visible: "The information used here was obtained free of charge from and is copyrighted by Retrosheet."
  • Also credit Lahman and SABR, Chadwick Bureau, the Library of Congress, Wikimedia and Wikipedia where they are used.
  • Keep the site static: no server, database, login or paid service without a pitch and your yes.
  • No outside fonts, scripts or CDNs on the live site without asking.
  • New data fields are optional, so old days still show. Every fetch needs error handling.
  • Be polite to outside sites: cache results and pace requests. MLB data is for individual, non-commercial, non-bulk use.
  • Do not copy data from sites whose terms bar it (Baseball-Reference, FanGraphs). Link to them.
  • Back up before editing. Nothing is committed, pushed or merged without your yes.
